# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This repository captures the project management practices OctoAcme uses to guide cross-functional work from idea to delivery. The goal is to make key workflows, roles, and decision points visible and repeatable so that projects can be initiated, planned, executed, and closed with less ambiguity and less dependence on tribal knowledge. These docs provide a lightweight but practical framework for managing projects with clear ownership, measurable outcomes, regular communication, and disciplined execution.

OctoAcme’s approach follows a simple lifecycle: initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective. During initiation, teams validate the business need, define success metrics, identify stakeholders, and decide whether to move forward with planning. Planning translates the approved initiative into a backlog, timeline, milestones, dependencies, and quality expectations. Execution then turns priorities into incremental, testable work through daily coordination, clear acceptance criteria, and visible progress tracking. Once features are ready, release activities focus on deployment readiness, smoke testing, and stakeholder communication, with rollback plans in place for critical issues. After each milestone or sprint, retrospectives capture lessons learned and turn them into improvements for the next cycle.

The process model is built around cross-functional collaboration. Product managers define outcomes and prioritize the roadmap, project managers coordinate schedules, risks, and communication, and developers and QA partners deliver the work against clear acceptance criteria. Stakeholders provide input and approvals, while the team uses transparent artifacts such as a project charter, backlog, risk register, and definition of done to keep scope and expectations aligned. Communication is intentionally regular: standups keep delivery on track, weekly syncs review risks and progress, and milestone demos or stakeholder updates keep everyone informed.

Quality is treated as part of the delivery process rather than a final checkpoint. The team is expected to use automated tests, linting, security scans, and smoke testing where appropriate, and to validate significant changes through manual QA when needed. Risk management is ongoing, including proactive issue identification, ongoing monitoring, and escalation paths when blockers affect milestones or customer value. The retrospective and continuous improvement practices help capture wins, identify gaps, and convert action items into concrete follow-up work, strengthening the team’s ability to learn and improve over time.

## How these docs fit together

These documents are meant to be used as a connected set rather than isolated checklists. The initiation guide starts the process, planning turns strategy into execution-ready work, execution keeps delivery moving, and release plus retrospectives close the loop. Together, they give the team a practical, repeatable operating model for delivering high-value work with accountability, transparency, and continuous learning.
